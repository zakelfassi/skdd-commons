# Hire the Circle

Values into scorecards, scorecards into interviews, and the circle's rings
staffed across humans and agents — with the two non-delegables in writing.

## Gate
None — team design is legitimate at any stage. But the output lands in
`GROWTH.md ## Team`, so create the file from the template if absent.

## Inputs
- `GROWTH.md` (what work the rings currently demand), current team and
  agent inventory, and the role being hired or the lane being delegated.

## The traits (score evidence, not vibes)
1. **Horsepower** — can actually do the analysis, not just commission it.
2. **Purpose over pressure** — won't buckle into short-term optimization.
3. **Competitive, idea-brutal, person-respectful** — attacks the claim,
   never the colleague.
4. **Quality bar near perfectionism** — a win of 10 basis points asks for 20.
5. **Courage to reset** — can take something working and say "not here,
   start again."
6. **Integrity under score pressure** — reports the failed experiment plainly.
7. **Hires better than themselves** — and isn't threatened when it works.
8. **Value over perception** — builds the real thing rather than the
   narrative of the thing. The trait the whole system stands on.

## Steps

1. **Write the scorecard.** Per trait: what *evidence* looks like in this
   candidate's history (a busted-belief story with the data; a metric they
   retired; a hire who outgrew them). One trait = one story minimum.

2. **Build the interview kit.** Questions that elicit stories, with
   listen-fors and red flags. Red flags worth naming: metric talk with no
   user in the sentence; wins narrated without luck acknowledged; contempt
   for "boring" experiments; K-factor enthusiasm before retention exists.

3. **Map the rings.** For each ring, assign: owner, operators, and
   boundary. Agents excel at the monotony — instrumentation, lore queries,
   loop bookkeeping, ledger hygiene, probe suites. Humans hold judgment
   calls: keystone succession, context-reset verdicts, publication.

4. **Write the two non-delegables.** The **alienation test** and **consent
   decisions** never go to agents, regardless of capability. Every
   autonomous lane gets: a ledger it writes to, attribution on every entry,
   and a named human veto. Ambiguity here is where spam machines are born.

5. **Record.** Scorecard, kit, and ring map to `GROWTH.md ## Team`, dated.
   Revisit the map when the team or the model generation changes.

## Conventions
- Culture-capability conflicts get flagged explicitly: a brilliant
  candidate who optimizes perception fails the card, and the card wins.
- Score agents like hires: an agent lane earns autonomy by ledger history
  (clean pre-registrations, honest kills), not by model name.
- The growth owner must be senior enough to engage core product strategy —
  growth abstracted away from product value becomes channel management.

## Edge Cases
- **Solo founder, no hires planned.** The ring map is still the deliverable:
  founder + agents is a team, and the non-delegables still need writing down.
- **A failing growth hire.** Re-run the scorecard against observed behavior;
  the gap names the problem (often trait 8, hired on trait 1).
- **An agent lane wants more autonomy.** Grant it ring by ring against its
  ledger history; never grant the non-delegables, which is what "earned
  autonomy" tempts you toward.
